# Solo Hero Rules - Quick Reference
(Based on Scarlet Heroes and Black Streams Solo Heroes)

## Core Concept
These rules allow a single PC to face challenges designed for a party. The hero is tougher and deals damage differently than normal characters.

## Damage Conversion (Hero vs Foes)

When a HERO attacks a FOE, convert the damage roll to "harm dice":

| Damage Rolled | Harm Dealt |
|---------------|------------|
| 1 | 0 harm |
| 2-5 | 1 harm |
| 6-9 | 2 harm |
| 10+ | 4 harm |

- Most normal foes (goblins, orcs, bandits) have 1-2 HD = 1-2 harm to kill
- Tougher foes (ogres, trolls) have 4+ HD = 4+ harm to kill
- Each point of harm kills 1 HD worth of foes

### Example
Hero rolls 7 damage with a sword → 2 harm → kills 2 HD worth of goblins (2 goblins or one 2 HD creature)

## Fray Die

Heroes get a free "Fray Die" attack each round:
- Roll 1d8 at the end of your turn
- Apply harm to any foe of 1 HD or less within melee range
- This represents the hero's superior combat ability

| Fray Die Roll | Harm |
|---------------|------|
| 1 | 0 |
| 2-5 | 1 (kills one 1HD foe) |
| 6-8 | 2 (kills two 1HD foes) |

## Foes Attacking Heroes

Foes deal damage normally to heroes - the hero's extra toughness comes from their hit points being more meaningful.

A solo hero effectively has HP equal to a small party.

## Defying Death

When reduced to 0 HP:
1. Roll 1d20 + CON modifier + level
2. Result determines fate:
   - 1-5: Dead
   - 6-10: Mortally wounded (die in 1d6 rounds without aid)
   - 11-15: Knocked out (wake in 1d6 turns)
   - 16-20: Barely conscious (1 HP, can act at -2)
   - 21+: Second wind (regain 1d6 HP)

## Trait Checks (Simple Skill System)

For non-combat challenges:
1. Roll 2d8 + relevant modifier
2. Compare to difficulty:
   - 9: Easy
   - 11: Normal
   - 13: Hard
   - 15: Very Hard
   - 17: Heroic

## Oracle System (For Solo Play)

### Yes/No Questions
Roll 1d6:
- 1: No, and... (negative complication)
- 2-3: No
- 4-5: Yes
- 6: Yes, and... (positive bonus)

### How Well Did It Go?
When success is assured but degree matters:
- 1: Disaster
- 2-3: Poorly
- 4-5: Well
- 6: Brilliantly

## Adventure Design for Solo Play

### Scene Types
1. **Investigation** - Gather clues, talk to NPCs
2. **Action** - Combat, chases, physical challenges
3. **Conflict** - Social conflict, negotiations
4. **Transition** - Travel, rest, resupply

### Clues and Progress
- Place 3 clues in each adventure section
- Finding 1 clue: Partial information
- Finding 2 clues: Clear direction
- Finding 3 clues: Complete picture

### Threat Escalation
If the hero delays too long:
1. Warning signs appear
2. Minor setback occurs
3. Major setback occurs
4. Time runs out / villain succeeds

## Mob Rules (Fighting Groups)

When facing a mob of weak foes:
- Treat the mob as a single entity with combined HD
- Mob attacks at +2 for each 3 members beyond the first
- Each point of harm reduces the mob by that many HD

### Example
A mob of 6 goblins (6 HD total) attacks at +4 (+2 for each 3 goblins beyond first).
Hero deals 2 harm → 2 goblins drop → mob now 4 HD, attacks at +2.

## Reaction Rolls for Solo Play

| 2d6 + CHA mod | Reaction |
|---------------|----------|
| 2 or less | Hostile, attacks |
| 3-5 | Unfriendly, may attack |
| 6-8 | Neutral, uncertain |
| 9-11 | Friendly, inclined to help |
| 12+ | Enthusiastic, eager ally |

## Running NPCs in Solo Play

When an NPC's action is unclear:
1. Consider their motivation
2. Roll on the oracle
3. If still unclear, roll on Event Meaning tables (Mythic GME)

NPCs should:
- Pursue their own goals
- React logically to hero's actions
- Sometimes make mistakes
- Occasionally surprise the hero
